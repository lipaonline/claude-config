# Writing a carousel

Contents: structure → hooks → slide copy rules → CTA → caption → worked example.

## Structure (7–10 slides is the sweet spot)

| # | Slide | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cover | Stop the scroll. Promise + tension. Readable at thumbnail size. |
| 2 | Setup (optional) | Why this matters / who it's for / the mistake everyone makes. |
| 3–8 | Points | One idea per slide. Title = the idea, body = one proof or example. |
| 9 | Recap (optional) | List of the points in 3–6 words each. Makes the post saveable. |
| 10 | CTA | One action only: save, follow, comment a keyword, or link in bio. |

Three archetypes, pick one:
- **Listicle** — "N ways / mistakes / tools". Points are parallel and swappable.
- **Story** — before → turning point → after → lesson. Points are sequential, keep them short.
- **Teardown** — one example analysed step by step (a post, a landing page, an ad). Use `image` slides with captions.

## Hook formulas for the cover

Write 5 candidate hooks, pick the one with the most tension, show the top 3 to the user.

- **Number + promise**: "7 hooks that stop the scroll"
- **Contrarian**: "Stop posting every day"
- **Mistake**: "You're using carousels wrong"
- **Result + timeframe**: "0 → 10k followers in 90 days with one format"
- **Question they already ask**: "Why does nobody swipe past slide 2?"
- **Before/after**: "My carousel got 12 likes. This one got 1,400. The difference:"
- **Secret / behind the scenes**: "The checklist I run before every post"

Rules: ≤ 8 words in the title, one accent word wrapped in `**…**`, a sub line of ≤ 15 words that names the audience or the payoff. No emoji in the title.

## Slide copy rules

- Title ≤ 7 words, body ≤ 25 words. If it doesn't fit, split the slide.
- Titles are the idea, not a label: "Lead with the number" not "Tip 1".
- Bodies are proof: an example, a number, a before/after, a one-line how-to.
- Each slide must make sense alone (people screenshot single slides).
- End slides 2–8 with an open loop when natural ("…but that's not the biggest one").
- Numbers: use `stat` slides for one big number, never bury a stat in body text.
- Quotes: only real ones with a real author, or clearly labelled as paraphrase.
- Lists: 3–5 items, ≤ 8 words each, parallel grammar.
- Match the user's language and register. Don't translate their brand voice into "creator English".

## CTA slide

One action. Rank by what the user wants:
1. **Save** — for how-to content (algorithms reward saves).
2. **Follow** — for series ("one breakdown a week").
3. **Comment a keyword** — for lead magnets (DM automation).
4. **Link in bio / profile** — LinkedIn only puts links in comments; say so.

Never stack "like, save, share, follow". Button text ≤ 4 words.

## Caption

Deliver the caption with the slides, in a fenced block:

```
<Hook line, repeats or twists the cover — first 125 chars show before "more">

<2–5 short lines expanding the promise or giving the one takeaway>

<CTA line, same action as the last slide>

<3–8 hashtags on Instagram / TikTok; 0–3 on LinkedIn, at the end>
```

LinkedIn: no hashtags wall, no link in the body (put it in the first comment), line breaks every 1–2 sentences.

## Worked example (input → outline)

Input: "carousel for LinkedIn about why founders should write their own posts, tone direct, dark theme"

Outline delivered before writing the JSON:

1. cover — "Your ghostwriter is costing you **trust**" / sub: Why founder-written posts outperform polished ones.
2. point 01 — "People follow people, not copy" / body: 3 signals readers detect in 2 seconds.
3. stat — "2.4×" / label: more comments on posts with a **first-person** story (source).
4. point 02 — "Rough beats perfect" / body: a typo shows a human typed it at 7am.
5. point 03 — "Write for 20 minutes, not 2 hours" / body: the 3-line template.
6. list — "The 20-minute routine" / 4 items.
7. quote — one real quote, attributed.
8. cta — "Try it tomorrow morning" / button: Follow for the templates.
